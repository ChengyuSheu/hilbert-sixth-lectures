$\mathbb{R}^2$ — a pair collides at most once

$\mathbb{T}^2$ — same data, and they meet again

Velocity-set explorer — property (♣)

Shaded: velocities that carry a particle from the origin onto a target sphere of radius $\varepsilon$ after time $\Delta t$, on the torus. Sweep $\Delta t$ from short to long: the admissible set fragments into slivers around lattice directions and its measure collapses. Long flight times are strong constraints, not weak ones — that is the whole content of (♣).

Measurements

Why this had to be invented

In $\mathbb{R}^d$, after a collision $(v_i-v_j)\cdot\omega>0$: the pair separates and, moving in straight lines, separates forever. On $\mathbb{T}^d$ they wrap and meet again. Worse, the Burago–Ferleger–Kononenko bound — total collisions among $n$ spheres $\le C(n,d)$, uniformly — is false on the torus. The classical accounting collapses, and the replacement is: if a molecule is too big for the classical bound, some bond must be long, and long bonds gain nearly $\varepsilon^{d-1}$ instead of $\varepsilon^{1-}$.
